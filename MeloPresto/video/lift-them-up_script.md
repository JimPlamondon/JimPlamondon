# "LIFT THEM UP"

**A MeloPresto film.** :70
Draft 2.

---

## The form

One voice. Eleven cards. Seventy seconds.

The voiceover **never names anyone and never explains anything**. That is the whole trick of the film we're paying tribute to, and it's why seventeen people fit into a minute there: the narration is a hymn to a *type* of person, and the pictures and name cards carry the who. Draft 1 got this wrong — it gave every innovator an explanatory clause and ran to 2:45. This one runs :70 — ten seconds over the spot we're saluting, because we have eleven cards to land — and it gets there by letting the pictures do their share of the work.

Black and white throughout. Archival grain, engravings, plate photography, manuscript, patent linework. **Color arrives exactly once: the last shot.**

## Narration — clean read

**128 words**, ~:67 at an unhurried 115 wpm. Unhurried, warm, no salesmanship. Reading *to* someone.

> Here's to the crazy ones.
>
> The monks. The schoolmistresses. The patent clerks. The ones who wouldn't leave music alone.
>
> Who looked at a page of music and saw a bad interface. Who were told the notation was fine, the keyboard was fine, the tuning was settled — and redrew it anyway.
>
> You can ignore them for a century. You can lose their names. The one thing you can't do is un-know what they figured out.
>
> They made music simpler for people they'd never meet, and asked for nothing back.
>
> Because the ones crazy enough to redraw music are the ones who redraw it.
>
> Every Melodian sees farther because they lifted us up.
>
> So pay it forward. Spread the word. Lift somebody up.

## Sound

One drone from 0:00. Each innovator adds a voice to it — by the maintainers' card it's a ten-voice chord built in front of the audience without them noticing. Hayden is the exception: he doesn't add a voice, he **doubles Wicki's at the octave**. The same idea, sounding twice, ninety years apart. Schoenberg's voice arrives as the piece's only **modulation** rather than a new pitch: the one key change, on the card about key change.

At 0:53, when the color hits, the chord resolves. At 0:58 it stops being score and becomes **diegetic** — that chord is now coming from the instrument the hands are playing.

Composed original. No library cue.

## The last shot is the whole product demo

Two people, seven seconds, no feature names, no UI, no voiceover claim. One person plays. A second person watches, reaches in, and plays it correctly the first time.

If that shot works, the film needs no argument. If it doesn't work, no argument saves it. Shoot it first, before anything else is scheduled — everything upstream is decoration on this.

MeloPresto wordmark, in color, on black. Held.

No "Learn more." No button. The film already said what to do.

## Assets

Portraits and plates are public domain for Guido, Newton, Euler, Roualle de Boisgelou, Glover, Wicki, and Schoenberg — engravings, plate photography, manuscript pages. Where no likeness survives, the *work* is the portrait: Guido's staff, Roualle's crossed-out accidentals.

Bill Sethares, Andy Milne, and Anthony Prechtl are alive and are colleagues. Tell them they're in it before it posts — not for permission, but because it's a nice thing to be told, and because they'll have better source images than we'll find. Same for Werner Schweer and Michael Good, and for Brian Hayden if he can be reached.

Schoenberg's Chart of the Regions: draw our own from the relationships rather than lifting the plate out of *Structural Functions of Harmony* — ours needs to move and to match the film's ink-and-lattice look anyway.

## Open questions

1. **Roualle de Boisgelou is in, from a tracked file, not the paper.** `Specs/JiMStudent_Spec.md` §3.3 gives "first described by Roualle de Boisgelou in 1764," so the card reads that way — *described*, which is what the spec claims, not *invented*. Worth one look at the paper itself to confirm the two agree before we cut it.
2. **The *Hex Player* year.** Left off the card rather than guessed. `Nomenclature.md` cites Milne & Prechtl without one.
3. **Euler:** 1739 (*Tentamen*) or 1774 for the lattice — use whichever you defend elsewhere in the corpus.
4. **Sethares:** 1993 (*JASA*) is on the card; 1998 (*Tuning, Timbre, Spectrum, Scale*) is the better-known citation.
5. **Wavu.** The house term is on no card here; a first-time viewer has no wavu yet. Say the word and Euler's card becomes *the Tonnetz — the wavu*.
6. **Aside, not about the film.** Two specs cite "Sight-Reading Music Theory" as *JMTP* 2009 (`JiMStudent_Spec.md` §488, `JimmyShujaa_Spec.md` §686), while `Specs/RealTimeExport/data/sources.json` records `PAPER_INDEX.md` as marking it UNPUBLISHED. One of those is wrong. Doesn't touch this film — it has no citations — but it will bite something eventually.
